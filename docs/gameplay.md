# Gameplay

How each mode, move and rule plays. The controls are in the [README](../README.md#play), and [evidence.md](evidence.md) records the source behind each rule and which rules are authored.

## Modes

The game opens on the original's title screen — the T1 intro, its volume warning (3 seconds), the ALICUU logo (1.5 seconds) and the NEO TEAM F.K. credit, is skipped (authored). The title holds its logo over a cloudy backdrop until any key brings up its menu: SINGLE, VS and TEAM VS, the cursor starting on SINGLE and keeping its place from one visit to the next. Up or left moves it up, and down or right moves it down, wrapping, one move per press, and an attack key chooses; SINGLE starts story mode (below), VS opens the character select screen and TEAM VS its team form (TEAM VS, below). R starts the title over, and Character select in the Menu skips it. The select screen is that of the original's VS single mode, over its own animated backdrop; Escape on it returns to the title, on the VS, TEAM VS and story forms alike (elsewhere Escape pauses). The 1P arrow starts on Ryuko's cell and moves a cell at a time with A, D, W and S, wrapping at the grid's edges; a held direction repeats after 0.5 seconds, then every 0.05 seconds. Resting on a fighter stands their portrait at your side, shaking in with their name sliding up beside it; leaving a cell clears it for a tick. Any attack key chooses, flashing the arrow and recolouring the portrait in that key's palette (H, K, G and J pick four of each fighter's eight; a choice of a palette another portrait holds takes the next), and the same keys then move the 2P arrow, whose portrait stands on the right facing in. The GATE LOCK cells, SHIN-Z and the empty slots, show no portrait and can't be chosen. DoraEmon's cell, locked in the original, shows his face here and can be chosen, in VS, TEAM VS and story alike. When both are chosen the two panels slide shut, and 1.02 seconds after the second choice the match starts with the pair, each fighter and their projectiles and effects in the chosen colours, though the HUD faces keep their usual ones; with the same key on both sides, player 1 takes the next palette in the match. A won Vs. CPU match returns to the screen, which remembers where both arrows were; Character select in the Menu opens it at any time, and R starts it over. These settings apply at once and persist across resets; on Vs. CPU and during a story battle they grey out. The partner uses the same rules as the player; apart from the original CPU, its behaviors are authored for testing.

Beneath the practice settings the Menu's Cheats tab carries a **Cheat mode** switch, saved on the device alone and restored on return, its values kept but greyed out while the switch is off, grouped as follows. **You · 1P**: **HP ×** (×1–×100), **Damage ×** (×1–×100), **Max EN** (100–1000 in steps of 100, 1–10 stocks, shown with the actual stock-digit art), **Unlimited EN**, and a **Starting EN** grant — round 1's first active tick sets your stock to Max EN minus one with an empty gauge, later rounds keeping the native carryover. **Low HP · all fighters**: a **Low HP line** (0–100% of each fighter's effective maximum; the native line is 30%), a **Damage cut ×** (×0–×5, scaling how much every loss under the line cuts — ×0 none, a full 100% cutting everything) and **Supers at any HP · 1P only**, which forces or suppresses only your tabled desperation-super life checks (every fighter's tabled thresholds follow the line, only yours bypass; SIN's and SOU's idle stance changes move with the line but never bypass). **Partners & enemies**: **Partner HP ×** (×1–×100, your allies), **Enemy HP ×** (×1–×100) and **Enemy EN stocks ×** (×1–×5). **B.R. modes**: **No partner**, which seats a B.R. battle without its native ally, and per-kill **HP** and **EN** overrides (0–1000 native units; blank pays the source amounts, 0 skips the award) that replace only a B.R. kill bonus paid to you. A change never touches the battle under way: it applies from the next match you start, or the next battle of a story — except a summon, which alone takes the pending values when the battle's snapshot has cheats off (a battle begun with them on lends it the snapshot instead) — and a battle begun with cheats on — or joined by a summon — shows a small gold CHEAT tag under the round timer and marks the run as cheated — a mark that holds through the title and its demos until you pick a story route or start a match outside the story. Under cheats the fixed UNDER PW glow beneath each HUD life bar gives way to that fighter's real Low HP line: a gold tick at the mark, the strip washing red at or under it, a Damage cut label drawn above the portrait strip, and the same marker, wash and label drawn over each fighter's bar in the arena (the label alone clamped inside the canvas) — all of it back to the native glow with the cheats off. HP × sets your fighter's maximum and starting life, and Enemy HP × the same for every fighter hostile to you — the practice dummy, the Vs. CPU and TEAM VS opponents, every story enemy and a summoned enemy — while your story allies keep their own (Partner HP × scales them). Each scaled maximum carries its fighter's desperation share, scripted life checks and story reinforcement marks with it. A scripted take or heal on a fighter's own life — a drain pass, TEAM VS's carried-over health and a fallen enemy's KO reward among them — reads as its share of the bar, with a 1-point pass still moving one point and ALPHA's KO Boss revival keeping its balance; a reaction's life take counts as the striker's damage, so an enemy's take on you never grows with your bar. A check for exactly 1 life stays where the source put it; healing caps at the new maximum, a perfect round reads it, and a story route reads your ending life by its share of the bar, keeping every path open. Max EN caps your stock — the gauge counts up to ten — and Unlimited EN refills you to that cap as fast as you can spend it; positive EN gain follows each fighter's final cap (three stocks gain ×1, five ×2, ten ×4.5). Damage × multiplies only your own positive attack damage — strikes, projectiles and chip alike — never what you take, heal or gain. All cheat behavior is authored; none of it is recovered from the original. The Match tab's **Enemy HP bars** is a live option, separate from Cheat mode: it shows a life bar over every hostile fighter present, in every mode, at once and without touching combat, alongside the story allies' native bars.

## Story mode

SINGLE opens the select screen with only the 1P arrow. Fighters with a story can be chosen, SHIN-Z among them; DoraEmon too, though the original locks him out; the empty slots and the CPU-only fighters stay locked. DoraEmon's story, which the original lacks, follows SHIN-Z's: two duels, five brawls in which GOLF, ASURA and then Z-MEGA fight at his side, the grunt brawl, YINAN and AYA, ZENKA, ALPHA, and SHIN-Z in the secret last battle. A knockout loses the battle, KO RELOAD or not; only one before anyone has touched him in a brawl or the ZENKA and ALPHA battles goes unscored. His three-stock revival is off whenever a player controls him, in every mode including B.R. (Reactions): in his own story a knockout never revives him, playing out as an ordinary one or, where the story's globals hold 10 (its first duels and every battle after demo 8), as KO NORMAL CPU's point-scoring repeat chain that loses without revival (Reactions). About a second after the choice the fighter's story begins and runs step by step: battles, demos between them, and jumps that pick the path by how the last battle went (the 1P's life left, whether a round was lost or drawn). A native story battle seats the fighter and up to nine CPUs on its own stage, at the levels the story sets, with its own time limit (or none, when the timer reads 0 throughout) and rounds to win, and may hold reinforcements back until a seat falls or some seconds pass. Every CPU but the fighter's allies attacks them, and each player turns to the nearest one it fights. Each ally shows a small life bar under its feet, its life in digits to the left; red marks what a hit took, shown for a fifth of a second before it drains away a point every hundredth of a second. A round ends when the fighter falls, when anyone's points reach 100 (a KO scores the points the story sets), or at the time limit. Some enemies give the fighter life and power back as they fall, and, as in the original, every damaging hit on a fallen enemy scores that KO and its reward again. In some native battles a knocked-out ZAKU or PAC-HELO gets back up: after five seconds on the floor — twenty where their native round scripts arm the slower rise — unless the round is over, its life refills and it stands to fight on. This native script never runs on an authored B.R. route (below). The right-hand life bar, gauge, face and stock follow the one enemy left standing, else the last one the fighter struck or whose attack the fighter's met, and the round marks show the rounds to win. ROUND and FIGHT show only where the story asks for them, and the result reads YOU WIN or YOU LOSE (a draw keeps KO) before the logo wipes back in. The fighter keeps the colour their key chose, and each CPU, as it enters, takes the first colour no other seat holds, counting the seats earlier battles in the story filled, which keep theirs. As in the original, the select screen and the demos carry their settings into the battles after them: a battle that follows one which leaves the VS setting opens on the fighters' VS entrances, round 1 on the VS screen, and hits that have stronger VS versions play those, while demos that switch it off bring back the brawl entrances and the logo. ## TEAM VS

TEAM VS opens the select screen on its own TEAM VS backdrop, and each side picks three fighters, the same one more than once if it likes. The arrow moves as in VS, the hovered fighter's portrait standing at its side, and an attack key picks the fighter under it as the next member, in that key's palette, or the next no other pick holds, so all six members show different colours. Each of the first two picks leaves its portrait standing above the side's spot, the first higher; the third decides, and once both sides have, the match starts 1.02 seconds later. A key pressed on the tick the arrow moves picks nothing. The match pits each side's first members against each other in 60-second rounds, and after each round the side that lost sends in its next member at full life (a draw or double KO, both sides), keeping that seat's power and stock. The winner stays and skips its entrance, its life rounded up to the next fifth of the full bar: above four fifths it comes back full, and never below two fifths. A few fighters round in their own steps, Z-MEGA's at 70% and 40%, GOLF's, ASURA's, ZAKU's and PAC-HELO's at half. RARIO, winning below two fifths, comes back full, as one of his checks jumps to itself and the original deletes it. Player 2's members are the original CPU, each with its own table.

## Summoning

With Cheat mode on, **Ctrl + Backquote** during a live match opens the summon grid over the frozen fight: pick a side (enemy or partner), walk the fighter tiles with WASD or the arrows, and G, Enter or a click or tap confirms; Tab flips the side and Esc closes. Any fighter but TRAINING joins as a full CPU — an enemy at the far edge of the enemy side, a partner behind you facing the fight — fights for its side until the next round or battle, and shows a life bar (a summoned enemy's only with Enemy HP bars). Summons never decide the native result themselves — they hold no seat, score no story points and take no KO rewards — but their fighting still lands on it: a summoned striker's KO pays the step's points where the step directs — a striker-scored KO landing on a native of the striker's side (you, for a partner) — so a round can end sooner than it would without them. The first summon marks the run cheated at once — the CHEAT tag appears whatever the battle's snapshot says. This section, like the cheats, is authored; the source has no summoning.

## Session

**Music**, ticked by default, plays the stage's music — untick it in the Menu. As in the original, its track starts as round 1 does and loops through the match; SIN's, KAY's and DIDO's supers silence every sound, and the track starts over once they finish. Greek plays its other track from round 3, and MASA's and 3-TIMES's matches play no stage track. Changing modes, fighters or the stage, or R, restarts the match. Leaving the window pauses the match and clears held input; resume explicitly. Touch buttons support simultaneous movement and actions. Landscape is recommended on phones.

## Basics

Hold crouch while attacking or guarding for low poses; attack or guard during a jump for aerial poses. Every attack strikes from its recovered hitbox, and every pose is hit through its own recovered body box. Centre to centre against a standing opponent, the standing punch and kick reach about 260 and 280 arena pixels, the air kick about 150, and Kick 3's beam about 550. Crouching or walking narrows the body, so attacks reach a little less; a backdash cannot be hit once it leaves the ground, and a grab cannot be struck on its grab tick. Life is the source's 510 points. A hit takes the striking box's damage, 20% more against most attacking poses. A guard takes nothing, except chip from IORI, the drills, the axe and most super boxes: 20% of the strike, or 50% from ZENKA, 40% from ALPHA and 30% from DoraEmon, and chip can KO. A guard holds against a strike from either side, a cross-up's too, and a fighter already guarding keeps guarding however it faces. At 30% life (153 for Ryuko) or less, every loss shrinks to 60%, or the fighter's own share: 65% for Chung, Amiro, SIN, DIDO, SAKUYA, SOU and SWW, 55% for Kiki, ANMC P, HISO, GIGI, MASA, 3-TIMES, ZENKA, Z-MEGA, SHIN-Z, ZAKU, PAC-HELO, GOLF, ASURA, TTRO, ASTROMAN, RARIO and TRAINING, 50% for DoraEmon and 45% for ALPHA. Each of DoraEmon's hits loses 2% for every hit the victim has taken since it last recovered. From a combo's second hit on, each hit shows the victim's count and HIT at the struck spot, which glow and rise away within 0.35 seconds. When the opponent crosses over, a grounded fighter plays the recovered 100 ms standing or crouching turn before walking or idling again; any other input interrupts it. The crouching kick sweeps: a hit pops the victim into a short knockdown, which slides them back 30 pixels as every knockdown does, and get-up. Attacks finish before another can start, except through the recovered chains and cancels below. Landing interrupts aerial attacks, and an air hit or air guard ends the moment it lands. Airborne fighters land before their round-result pose. KO plays the recovered lettering sequence for 166 ticks (1.66 seconds); in free practice, one second with the winner visible follows before the result. R restarts the match; the floating Menu button at the top right opens options and Character select, keeping the ending poses unobscured.

## Standing chains

Punch and kick chain only after contact. When punch 1 or kick 1 hits or is blocked, it switches to its hit branch on the next tick; 180 ms later (the 100 ms contact pause plus the branch's first 80 ms pose) the branch looks back 200 ms (250 ms for the kick) for a fresh punch or kick press made after a moment with no direction held. Punch enters punch 2 and kick enters kick 2, so the chains cross. A whiff has no follow-up, and neither has a hit landing as the opponent's own box finds the attacker, even one that does no damage, as FENG's Evil IORI P3 does. A punch or kick that meets an object, such as YINAN's dash figure, takes its branch at once, even when player 1's hit lands on player 2 in the same moment; player 2's hit on player 1 still wins. Punch 2 checks the same way after each of its three strikes, and kick 2 once after its strike; a press there cancels at once into punch 3 or kick 3, even on a whiff. Punch 2 and kick 2 open with a lunge of about 117 and 99 arena pixels, which carries them back into range after the victim's slide. The second standing punch and jumping kick each have three strikes; the third standing kick has eight. Each strike has its own active window and hit/block effect, with inactive gaps between strikes. Their total damage stays at 10, 13, and 17 respectively. Holding an attack never repeats it, and a hit on the attacker ends its chain.

## Jumps and dashes

Jump direction is chosen when jump is pressed: Ryuko's neutral jumps rise about 318 arena pixels, while her forward/backward jumps rise about 296 and travel about 348 pixels when unobstructed. Every fighter walks at its own pace, most a little slower backing off, jumps with its own up, forward and back jumps, poses, height and speed, and drops from an air hit at its own weight (MASA fastest): 3-TIMES rises higher and falls faster, AMIRO takes off sooner going forward or back, and OS FENG's air fire drops the way he jumped. Jump travel continues after releasing direction and through aerial attacks or guard. Double jumps pause for 30 ms before launching in the newly selected direction. These velocities and acceleration use recovered actions 4–6 and 10–12, converted from the original fixed-point units to the arena scale. In the air, up, ↗ or ↖ pressed from neutral double jumps. A direction, neutral, then the same direction again within 130 ms dashes that way, on the ground or in the air, on keyboard or touch; Shift dashes forward, or backward while back is held. A jump allows one double jump and one air dash, and a double jump can still air dash; an air dash or a finished aerial attack allows neither until landing, and neither comes out within 50 source pixels of the floor on the way down. Ground dashes launch after 50/100 ms of preparation, hold their airborne pose until landing, and land through Dash F lend (action 15, 80 ms). Actions 13–14 set forward/backward velocities of +900/-500, upward velocities of -500/-900, and gravity 90 in source units. Unobstructed hops cover about 243/247 arena pixels in 190/340 ms, respectively; the backward hop is higher and slower. Air dashes retain the incoming jump or fall motion during their 30 ms preparation; if that fall reaches the floor, they land before launching. At launch they replace it with level motion at about 12 arena pixels per tick: 300 ms of level travel forward (about 360 pixels), or 200 ms backward (about 240 pixels). Their 100 ms recovery restores gravity while keeping horizontal velocity; that drift continues through the fall that follows until landing, and a guard waits for the landing, since the fall is still the dash's (action 7, entered by its jump). A dash that reaches the floor before that fall ends at once, free. Actions 16–17 supply velocity ±800 and gravity 80; the recovery motion flag `0x02` retains horizontal velocity, as does the following fall action 7. A forward air dash near the neutral-jump apex covers about 720 arena pixels through landing when unobstructed. The 960-pixel viewport scrolls over a 1920-pixel stage, so crossing the old screen edge no longer cuts off the move. Actual stage walls and body collision can shorten travel.

## Drill Punch

Ground Drill Punch lunges from action ticks 11–59 (110–590 ms before contact pauses) at about 13.5 arena pixels per tick, slides at about 1.5 pixels per tick until action tick 69, then stops for its remaining recovery. Contact pauses stop this movement clock as well as the poses. Body collisions and arena walls limit its travel. It advances through six timed strikes for 3 damage each (18 total), with hit reactions and sparks on every contact; the sixth strike launches the victim. Guard blocks all six strikes. The aerial drill holds its 220 ms startup, then dives forward at about 12 arena pixels per tick and downward at about 15; follow-up stages increase the downward speed to about 22.5. It spins until landing. Hit or block advances its contact stages; a missed spin stays armed. Damage drops from 16 to 5, 3, then 1 per subsequent contact, so its hit count depends on contact and airtime; a 1-damage spin that passes without contact returns to 3. A hit slams the victim down into a floor bounce, and an airborne victim is struck again by each stage until it lands. From the tick after contact the attacker holds position for eight ticks, the last of which moves, then two more reach the next strike, 110 ms per stage. Landing ends the attack and plays its recovery. Its spinning box reaches about 189 arena pixels ahead.

## Axe throw

Axe throw releases a spinning axe that appears 170 ms in, flying at about 13.5 arena pixels per tick; pressing it again while that axe flies only makes Ryuko stand for a moment. It hits through body boxes like any attack (36 against an attacking pose), so it passes through a fighter mid-throw and under a high enough jump, and it can be blocked. Two opposing axes break each other where they meet, the Mega Getter Beam's beam breaks every opposing axe, and its lunge meeting an axe enters its hit cinematic. A super's freeze holds a flying axe in place without its hitbox, and an axe vanishes 75 pixels past either end of the stage. These two basic specials are meter-free.

## Hit Up

Press K or the Hit Up touch button on the ground for an upward kick. It steps forward about 120 arena pixels, strikes after 150 ms of action time, and deals 15 damage. A grounded hit pauses the attacker for 330 ms and the victim for 100 ms; the victim then holds the impact pose for 250 ms before rising about 589 arena pixels and falling into knockdown/get-up. Press or hold Jump in the last 150 ms of the contact pause or the first 50 ms after it to chase them, then use an aerial attack; an earlier press is too early. The chase prepares for 80 ms and preserves its forward momentum in the air. Blocking or missing plays the full recovery without a chase. An airborne victim instead takes a shorter hold and a stronger outward launch, and so does one still reeling from an ICE Punch (SWW's, OS FENG's, SAKUYA's or DIDO's), even on the ground. The camera tracks the pair's midpoint as always, rising once the launch lifts it high enough, and the HUD stays fixed. The K binding is a training choice.

## IORI

Press F or the IORI touch button on the ground for a three-part advancing attack. Each stage checks for IORI's ↓← and G (F enters them too) three times after its strike: at 290, 490 and 540 ms of its own time for the first, 310, 510 and 560 ms for the second, each looking back 300 ms (200 ms at the last). A match starts the next stage at once. Holding the button performs one stage. You can continue on hit, block, or whiff. After contact, a press early in the hit pause can be too old: the cancel window's command checks clear all but the last 200 ms of input each time they find IORI's command. Damage is 15 + 18 + 20 (53 total); a guard takes 3 + 3 + 4 chip. The first grounded hit pushes away, the second pulls inward, and the finisher launches a grounded victim away or slams an airborne victim into a bounce and knockdown. Without follow-up input, each stage completes its recovery. Once a stage connects, a super cancels it (see below). The F binding is a training choice.

## Daisetsuzan Oroshi

Press ←↓→ and H on the ground for Ryuko's command throw. 40 ms into the move, its grab box reaches an opponent up to about 259 arena pixels away, including one reeling from a hit, but not one blocking or turning. Started farther than Ryuko's near distance (about 165 arena pixels), the command plays DST FAR instead, whose shorter box reaches only 169 but stays open through the whole move: DST FAR never clears it, so it grabs an opponent who steps in during its recovery. A miss plays a 580 ms recovery. On a grab, Ryuko spins the opponent overhead for 1.8 seconds, then hurls them into the sky for 10 damage. As they fall, Ryuko slides back and fires two missiles: the first catches them for 22 damage, and the second flies past. The missile hit spins them away into a bounce, knockdown, and get-up. The held opponent cannot be hit. Time running out doesn't stop the spin: the toss still lands. A lethal toss still finishes Ryuko's sequence before the victory pose. At 30% health or less, the command performs EVIL DST instead: the same grab and miss recovery with its own poses, then five held strikes (1.65 seconds, 25 power, no damage) before the usual spin, toss and missiles.

Press E or the EX mode touch button on the ground to enter EX mode for 50 power (half a stock), and again to leave it for another 50; with less, Ryuko plays NO SUPER. Ryuko flashes for 200 ms, and her HUD face blinks an EX mark while EX mode lasts. In EX mode, ↓→ and G fires EX Fire instead of the axe. Holding forward charges it, checked every 10–50 ms from 180 ms after the press. Let go before 510 ms for a 30-damage shot, a little slower than the axe, or later for a faster, 40-damage one. Holding through 770 ms fires a large 50-damage shot that launches the opponent, with a bigger recoil. As with the axe, a second press while a shot is out only stands for a tick. Opposing shots and axes break each other. In EX mode ↓→↓→ and G on the ground fires EFS instead of Getter Beam: four shots and a launching final one for a stock, or two when it cancels IORI. In Vs. CPU, holding down as the round's intro reaches its last poses starts the round in EX mode. ## Power and supers

Hits, blocks, and damage taken fill the power gauge at the bottom of the screen. Each full gauge becomes a stock, up to three for most fighters: ZAKU and PAC-HELO hold one, GOLF and ASURA two, ZENKA, Z-MEGA, ALPHA and DoraEmon five, and SHIN-Z six, and DoraEmon's stocks take 90 power each. There is no free meter in practice. The supers' motion commands spend stock. Without enough stock, Ryuko plays NO SUPER for 400 ms and gains 10 power instead. Each super freezes the opponent while Ryuko transforms, and most projectiles and blasts on screen, even one partway through a run of strikes, hold their pose without striking until the freeze ends. A fighter's first KO freezes them too, for half a second. Getter Beam fires a screen-long beam that hits 20 times for 100 damage; a guard takes 1 chip from each. Pinned against a wall, the opponent's slides push Ryuko back, and the beam moves with her. At 30% health or less it becomes the stronger desperation beam (120 damage). In the air, Ryuko hovers while firing, then falls. Musashi and the Getter Change Attack are unblockable grabs, though both pass over an opponent still reeling from a guard, and the Change Attack also over one in the air or reeling from a hit: Musashi lifts the opponent overhead and slams them for 120 damage. Lifted against a wall, the opponent stays at the wall and pushes Ryuko back instead. Cancelled from IORI, Musashi also catches an opponent in the air. The Change Attack repeats the Daisetsuzan spin, tosses the opponent, catches them in the sky, throws them and fires a beam as they fall. ↓←↓→ and G throws an axe until Ryuko's health falls below 30%, then performs the seven-strike Black Getter Mega Rush for 125 damage. The Mega Getter Beam needs all three stocks: its opening blast shocks the opponent, then a cinematic beam from the sky hits 40 times; 240 damage in all. A hit on Ryuko cancels her super, including a beam already on screen.

## Reactions

Every attack chooses its victim's reaction from the recovered response table: standing, crouching or airborne, each guarded or not. A victim frozen by a hit counts as crouching only if down was held when the freeze began. A damaging hit ends the freezes of the victim and of whatever struck it at once, so both move on the tick it lands. Struck, the victim falls one step behind its attacker and acts first each tick, so a fighter's own hit freeze ends 10 ms before its victim's, and a button pressed as the hit lands is too old to chain once that freeze ends. Air punch and air kick are overheads, hitting a crouching guard and pushing a standing guard back. Punch 3 slams any grounded victim into a floor bounce, even through a crouching guard, landing into a knockdown that lies 300 ms less for every fighter but Ryuko and LIZARD; once the bounce starts, attacks that reach its low body (kicks, crouching attacks, IORI) strike the victim again. An air guard stops only aerial attacks, punch 3, IORI, the drill and the axe; the ground normals and Hit Up hit it. Each grounded reaction then slides as in the source: a light hit about 84 arena pixels, a heavy hit 214, an air attack's hit 71 (217 from the sixth in a row before it recovers, 363 from the ninth), a light block 96 and a heavy block about 204. A beam hit (kick 3, Getter Beam) pushes the victim back for 25 ticks, then drifts it in again: about 12 pixels between kick 3's strikes and 18 between Getter Beam's. Air hits rise briefly, then fly the victim up and away; an air guard slides back, speeding up, as it falls. An air reaction that lands ends at once, so the fighter can act on the landing tick; one that runs out in the air becomes a normal fall. A few lethal blows make their victim vanish instead of falling: LIZARD's chuck sup, HISO's FISH, SEAN's SCC and G Suneo's hh, GP and en300. The screen flashes faintly and the KO'd fighter disappears; TRAINING is knocked flying instead, gets up and drops into his KO. Once a round, outside the select screen's VS matches, ALPHA survives a KO while he holds a stock — except on a B.R. route, where nothing revives (below): flung away at 1 life, he lies down, then draws his life back to full, spending his power; his next KO that round is final. DoraEmon survives one with three stocks the same way — as a CPU outside the B.R. routes, where no seat revives (below); played by you a KO never revives him however many stocks he holds, in every mode including B.R.: an ordinary one in practice, VS, TEAM VS and the B.R. routes, and in his own story an ordinary one or, where the story's globals hold 10 (its first duels and every battle after demo 8), KO NORMAL CPU's repeat chain, which loses without revival. The chain is not the CPU's alone — it plays for his fighter too, his KO repeating every tick, each scoring the story's points for it, until about a hundred have passed; a CPU outside B.R. runs it only where global 3 stands at 10 with fewer than three stocks. After some story scenes, among them the one before the story's MASA battle, MASA and 3-TIMES can't be struck again once KO'd.

Before any hit lands, attack boxes of opposite sides that overlap are checked against each other. Two that both deal no damage cancel each other, and boxes with damage pass through, except into a guard point: TTRO's P and ASTROMAN's P, K, Punch and SUPER P open a harmless box over the body, and a damaging box that meets it is spent while the guard counters (TTRO's P gard freezes the attacker, then swipes; ASTROMAN's SP GARDs each power SUPER P up a level, LV1 to LV4, 130 to 170, deepening his red tint). Kiki's potatoes break the same way when a box meets theirs.

In a story brawl, a KO bonus can restore life to a fighter who has fallen. Outside B.R., if the round is still open and their KO Down's own life check finds at least 1 HP, they recover on that fighter's check tick; TRAINING first enters its knockdown. The KO still counts for brawl elimination. A bonus paid in the same tick as a trade KO can save the 1P before a loss is picked, but one paid after the result starts cannot undo it. This life-check recovery is disabled in B.R., and so is every refill: on a B.R. route no scripted or bonus gain raises a fighter already down, DoraEmon's KO RELOAD stays closed for every seat, ALPHA's KO Boss revive jump never plays, and ZAKU's and PAC-HELO's knockdowns take the ordinary lying KO instead of their five-second revival — a knocked-out fighter stays down for the rest of the battle. Every rule here is unchanged outside the B.R. routes.

## DoraEmon's move protection and entrance

Twin Hammer protects him from strikes and grabs during the cast, but its hit follow-up is vulnerable. Fists' active punches clash with opposing strikes and projectiles, clearing that exchange; the next punch can clash again, while his body remains vulnerable to attacks that miss the punch. Pierce protects against strikes and grabs for the first second from casting, including time spent in hit-stop, while he remains in the move. Leaving or super-cancelling out ends that protection. For Counter Hit, press K during any standing or crouching block with a stock; an air guard cannot counter.

When DoraEmon uses his Br. entrance, a waiting-seat arrival plays the same 337-tick sequence as a first-seat entrance: he rises, falls and lands, gains four 90-power stocks once, and cannot be hit or pushed while in the intro. His CPU resumes after the entrance. This intro charge is separate from the voluntary EN command's existing availability restrictions.

## Cancels

Specials and supers cancel an attack once its own strike has hit or been blocked: punch, kick, the crouching attacks, punch 2 and kick 2 accept the drill, IORI, DST, the axe and every super. Punch 1's and kick 1's hit branches accept them at once. A connection stays counted until a move or reaction plays out: struck in the air after your strike connected, you land still counted, so the next of those attacks takes a special or super before its own strike comes out. The press counts on any tick, including the contact pause, as long as it is still within its command's window when the pause ends; the new move starts then. A connected air punch accepts the air kick, Air Drill, the air Getter Beam or a double jump; a connected air kick accepts the same except the air punch. Air attacks after a double jump or during an air dash cannot double-jump cancel; air dashes accept air attacks and the air specials from their fourth tick, and those attacks fall more slowly. A connected IORI stage accepts only the supers' commands but the Mega Getter Beam's: this super cancel plays the SC version, costs two stocks (NO SUPER otherwise) and flashes GENGAI!, and the hidden super's cancel plays NO SUPER at 150 of 510 life or more.

## COUNTER HIT

While a standing or crouching guard reaction plays and Ryuko has a stock, press K to COUNTER HIT: she spends the stock, freezes the attacker and swings a wide blow that launches them. The press counts at the guard reaction's recovered checks, including one up to 80 ms before the block. Air guards cannot counter.

## Throws

Press J or the Throw touch button to grab an opponent, including one holding back to guard. The grab checks after 70 ms: its box reaches a standing, crouching or attacking opponent on the ground up to about 167 arena pixels away, but not one airborne, turning, reeling from a hit or blocking. A miss plays a 620 ms recovery. Ryuko cannot be struck on the grab tick, so a punch landing then passes through her, and two simultaneous grabs both miss. A grab holds the opponent in front for 50 ms and spins them through three 200 ms cycles, as the Daisetsuzan's first spins do, then tosses them into the air for 30 damage. Ryuko's recovery lasts 1.4 seconds. The opponent tumbles, bounces into knockdown and gets up before control returns. The held opponent cannot be hit; the tossed one can, until knockdown (its 129–131 body boxes are kind 2). A lying fighter's body sits below the floor and a rising one has none, so neither can be hit or grabbed. Knockdown and get-up keep push boxes, so bodies still push apart. Z-MEGA and SHIN-Z can rise into a counter, RAMDOM GET UP: if the opponent stands within about 450 and 510 arena pixels in front as they start to get up, the screen darkens and a blow strikes for 40 and 50. SHIN-Z counters five times in six, Z-MEGA half the time. It costs no power and works in every mode, free practice included. Fighters stay 75 arena pixels inside the view's edges, as the engine clamps them, so a stage end's wall is 75 pixels in; a wide pose keeps its anchor when the pose changes. A hit on the thrower before the toss leaves the opponent held until the thrower's hit stop has counted down, then it stands free where it was held, without the toss's damage. A lethal toss KOs the victim where it was tossed; reset releases held fighters.
