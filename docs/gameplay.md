# Gameplay

How each mode, move and rule plays. The controls are in the [README](../README.md#play), and [evidence.md](evidence.md) records the source behind each rule and which rules are authored.

## Session

Tick **Music** above the arena for the stage's track, which the original keeps silent in these matchups. Changing modes, fighters or the stage, or Reset, restarts the match. Leaving the window pauses the match and clears held input; resume explicitly. Touch buttons support simultaneous movement and actions. Landscape is recommended on phones.

## Basics

Hold crouch while attacking or guarding for low poses; attack or guard during a jump for aerial poses. Every attack strikes from its recovered hitbox, and every pose is hit through its own recovered body box. Centre to centre against a standing opponent, the standing punch and kick reach about 260 and 280 arena pixels, the air kick about 150, and Kick 3's beam about 550. Crouching or walking narrows the body, so attacks reach a little less; a backdash cannot be hit once it leaves the ground, and a grab cannot be struck on its grab tick. Life is the source's 510 points. A hit takes the striking box's damage, 20% more against most attacking poses. A guard takes nothing, except 20% chip from IORI, the drills, the axe and most super boxes, and chip can KO. At 30% life (153) or less, every loss shrinks to 60%. When the opponent crosses over, a grounded fighter plays the recovered 100 ms standing or crouching turn before walking or idling again; any other input interrupts it. The crouching kick sweeps: a hit pops the victim into a short knockdown and get-up. Attacks finish before another can start, except through the recovered chains and cancels below. Landing interrupts aerial attacks, and an air hit or air guard ends the moment it lands. Airborne fighters land before their round-result pose. KO plays the recovered lettering sequence for 166 ticks (1.66 seconds); in free practice, one second with the winner visible follows before the result. Rematch controls appear below the court, keeping the ending poses unobscured.

## Standing chains

Punch and kick chain only after contact. When punch 1 or kick 1 hits or is blocked, it switches to its hit branch on the next tick; 180 ms later (the 100 ms contact pause plus the branch's first 80 ms pose) the branch looks back 200 ms (250 ms for the kick) for a fresh punch or kick press made after a moment with no direction held. Punch enters punch 2 and kick enters kick 2, so the chains cross. A whiff has no follow-up. Punch 2 checks the same way after each of its three strikes, and kick 2 once after its strike; a press there cancels at once into punch 3 or kick 3, even on a whiff. Punch 2 and kick 2 open with a lunge of about 117 and 99 arena pixels, which carries them back into range after the victim's slide. The second standing punch and jumping kick each have three strikes; the third standing kick has eight. Each strike has its own active window and hit/block effect, with inactive gaps between strikes. Their total damage stays at 10, 13, and 17 respectively. Holding an attack never repeats it, and a hit on the attacker ends its chain.

## Jumps and dashes

Jump direction is chosen when jump is pressed: neutral jumps rise about 318 arena pixels, while forward/backward jumps rise about 296 and travel about 348 pixels when unobstructed. Jump travel continues after releasing direction and through aerial attacks or guard. Double jumps pause for 30 ms before launching in the newly selected direction. These velocities and acceleration use recovered actions 4–6 and 10–12, converted from the original fixed-point units to the arena scale. In the air, up, ↗ or ↖ pressed from neutral double jumps. A direction, neutral, then the same direction again within 130 ms dashes that way, on the ground or in the air, on keyboard or touch; Shift dashes forward, or backward while back is held. A jump allows one double jump and one air dash, and a double jump can still air dash; an air dash or a finished aerial attack allows neither until landing, and neither comes out within 50 source pixels of the floor on the way down. Ground dashes launch after 50/100 ms of preparation, hold their airborne pose until landing, and land through Dash F lend (action 15, 80 ms). Actions 13–14 set forward/backward velocities of +900/-500, upward velocities of -500/-900, and gravity 90 in source units. Unobstructed hops cover about 243/247 arena pixels in 190/340 ms, respectively; the backward hop is higher and slower. Air dashes retain the incoming jump or fall motion during their 30 ms preparation; if that fall reaches the floor, they land before launching. At launch they replace it with level motion at about 12 arena pixels per tick: 300 ms of level travel forward (about 360 pixels), or 200 ms backward (about 240 pixels). Their 100 ms recovery restores gravity while keeping horizontal velocity; that drift continues through the fall that follows until landing, and the guard key waits for the landing, since the fall is still the dash's (action 7, entered by its jump). A dash that reaches the floor before that fall ends at once, free. Actions 16–17 supply velocity ±800 and gravity 80; the recovery motion flag `0x02` retains horizontal velocity, as does the following fall action 7. A forward air dash near the neutral-jump apex covers about 720 arena pixels through landing when unobstructed. The 960-pixel viewport scrolls over a 1920-pixel stage, so crossing the old screen edge no longer cuts off the move. Actual stage walls and body collision can shorten travel.

## Drill Punch

Ground Drill Punch lunges from action ticks 11–59 (110–590 ms before contact pauses) at about 13.5 arena pixels per tick, slides at about 1.5 pixels per tick until action tick 69, then stops for its remaining recovery. Contact pauses stop this movement clock as well as the poses. Body collisions and arena walls limit its travel. It advances through six timed strikes for 3 damage each (18 total), with hit reactions and sparks on every contact; the sixth strike launches the victim. Guard blocks all six strikes. The aerial drill holds its 220 ms startup, then dives forward at about 12 arena pixels per tick and downward at about 15; follow-up stages increase the downward speed to about 22.5. It spins until landing. Hit or block advances its contact stages; a missed spin stays armed. Damage drops from 16 to 5, 3, then 1 per subsequent contact, so its hit count depends on contact and airtime; a 1-damage spin that passes without contact returns to 3. A hit slams the victim down into a floor bounce, and an airborne victim is struck again by each stage until it lands. From the tick after contact the attacker holds position for eight ticks, the last of which moves, then two more reach the next strike, 110 ms per stage. Landing ends the attack and plays its recovery. Its spinning box reaches about 189 arena pixels ahead.

## Axe throw

Axe throw releases a spinning axe that appears 170 ms in, flying at about 13.5 arena pixels per tick; pressing it again while that axe flies only makes Ryuko stand. The command stays in its 400 ms window, so she keeps standing until the axe's hit stops her, and throws another if the window is still open then. It hits through body boxes like any attack (36 against an attacking pose), so it passes through a fighter mid-throw and under a high enough jump, and it can be blocked. Two opposing axes break each other where they meet, the Mega Getter Beam's beam breaks every opposing axe, and its lunge meeting an axe enters its hit cinematic. A super's freeze holds a flying axe in place without its hitbox, and an axe vanishes 75 pixels past either end of the stage. These two basic specials are meter-free.

## Hit Up

Press H or the Hit Up touch button on the ground for an upward kick. It steps forward about 120 arena pixels, strikes after 150 ms of action time, and deals 15 damage. A grounded hit pauses the attacker for 330 ms and the victim for 100 ms; the victim then holds the impact pose for 250 ms before rising about 589 arena pixels and falling into knockdown/get-up. Press or hold Jump in the last 150 ms of the contact pause or the first 50 ms after it to chase them, then use an aerial attack; an earlier press is too early. The chase prepares for 80 ms and preserves its forward momentum in the air. Blocking or missing plays the full recovery without a chase. An airborne victim instead takes a shorter hold and a stronger outward launch. The camera tracks the pair's midpoint as always, rising once the launch lifts it high enough, and the HUD stays fixed. The H binding is a training choice.

## IORI

Press F or the IORI touch button on the ground for a three-part advancing attack. Each stage checks for IORI's ↓← and J (F enters them too) three times after its strike: at 290, 490 and 540 ms of its own time for the first, 310, 510 and 560 ms for the second, each looking back 300 ms (200 ms at the last). A match starts the next stage at once. Holding the button performs one stage. You can continue on hit, block, or whiff. After contact, a press early in the hit pause can be too old: the cancel window's command checks clear all but the last 200 ms of input each time they find IORI's command. Damage is 15 + 18 + 20 (53 total); a guard takes 3 + 3 + 4 chip. The first grounded hit pushes away, the second pulls inward, and the finisher launches a grounded victim away or slams an airborne victim into a bounce and knockdown. Without follow-up input, each stage completes its recovery. Once a stage connects, a super cancels it (see below). The F binding is a training choice.

## Daisetsuzan Oroshi

Press G or the Daisetsuzan touch button on the ground for Ryuko's command throw. 40 ms into the move, its grab box reaches an opponent up to about 259 arena pixels away, including one reeling from a hit, but not one blocking or turning. Started farther than Ryuko's near distance (about 165 arena pixels), the command plays DST FAR instead, whose shorter box reaches only 169 but stays open through the whole move: DST FAR never clears it, so it grabs an opponent who steps in during its recovery. A miss plays a 580 ms recovery. On a grab, Ryuko spins the opponent overhead for 1.8 seconds, then hurls them into the sky for 10 damage. As they fall, Ryuko slides back and fires two missiles: the first catches them for 22 damage, and the second flies past. The missile hit spins them away into a bounce, knockdown, and get-up. The held opponent cannot be hit. Time running out doesn't stop the spin: the toss still lands. A lethal toss still finishes Ryuko's sequence before the victory pose. At 30% health or less, G performs EVIL DST instead: the same grab and miss recovery with its own poses, then five held strikes (1.65 seconds, 25 power, no damage) before the usual spin, toss and missiles.

## EX mode

Press E or the EX mode touch button on the ground to enter EX mode for 50 power (half a stock), and again to leave it for another 50; with less, Ryuko plays NO SUPER. Ryuko flashes for 200 ms, and her HUD face blinks an EX mark while EX mode lasts. In EX mode, I fires EX Fire instead of the axe. Holding forward charges it, checked every 10–50 ms from 180 ms after the press. Let go before 510 ms for a 30-damage shot, a little slower than the axe, or later for a faster, 40-damage one. Holding through 770 ms fires a large 50-damage shot that launches the opponent, with a bigger recoil. As with the axe, a second press while a shot is out only stands for a tick. Opposing shots and axes break each other. Key 1 on the ground fires EFS instead of Getter Beam: four shots and a launching final one for a stock, or two when it cancels IORI. In Vs. CPU, holding down as the round's intro reaches its last poses starts the round in EX mode. Chung's, Amiro's, ANMC P's, Giant's, G Suneo's, SIN's, KAY's, SAKUYA's, ALEX's, SOU's and YINAN's EX modes work the same way: E or ↓ ↓ H for 50 power each way, the HUD face blinking its EX mark, and down held as the intro ends.

## Power and supers

Hits, blocks, and damage taken fill the power gauge at the bottom of the screen. Each full gauge becomes a stock, up to three for most fighters: ZAKU and PAC-HELO hold one, GOLF and ASURA two, ZENKA, Z-MEGA, ALPHA and DoraEmon five, and SHIN-Z six, and DoraEmon's stocks take 90 power each. There is no free meter in practice. Keys 1–5 or the gold touch buttons spend stock on the supers. Without enough stock, Ryuko plays NO SUPER for 400 ms and gains 10 power instead. Each super freezes the opponent while Ryuko transforms. Getter Beam fires a screen-long beam that hits 20 times for 100 damage; a guard takes 1 chip from each. Pinned against a wall, the opponent's slides push Ryuko back, and the beam moves with her. At 30% health or less it becomes the stronger desperation beam (120 damage). In the air, Ryuko hovers while firing, then falls. Musashi and the Getter Change Attack are unblockable grabs, though both pass over an opponent still reeling from a guard, and the Change Attack also over one in the air or reeling from a hit: Musashi lifts the opponent overhead and slams them for 120 damage. Lifted against a wall, the opponent stays at the wall and pushes Ryuko back instead. Cancelled from IORI, Musashi also catches an opponent in the air. The Change Attack repeats the Daisetsuzan spin, tosses the opponent, catches them in the sky, throws them and fires a beam as they fall. Key 4 throws an axe until Ryuko's health falls below 30%, then performs the seven-strike Black Getter Mega Rush for 125 damage. The Mega Getter Beam needs all three stocks: its opening blast shocks the opponent, then a cinematic beam from the sky hits 40 times; 240 damage in all. A hit on Ryuko cancels her super, including a beam already on screen.

## Reactions

Every attack chooses its victim's reaction from the recovered response table: standing, crouching or airborne, each guarded or not. A victim frozen by a hit counts as crouching only if down was held when the freeze began. A damaging hit ends the freezes of the victim and of whatever struck it at once, so both move on the tick it lands. Air punch and air kick are overheads, hitting a crouching guard and pushing a standing guard back. Punch 3 slams any grounded victim into a floor bounce, even through a crouching guard; once the bounce starts, attacks that reach its low body (kicks, crouching attacks, IORI) strike the victim again. An air guard stops only aerial attacks, punch 3, IORI, the drill and the axe; the ground normals and Hit Up hit it. Each grounded reaction then slides as in the source: a light hit about 84 arena pixels, a heavy hit 214, an air attack's hit 71, a light block 96 and a heavy block about 204. A beam hit (kick 3, Getter Beam) pushes the victim back for 25 ticks, then drifts it in again: about 12 pixels between kick 3's strikes and 18 between Getter Beam's. Air hits rise briefly, then fly the victim up and away; an air guard slides back, speeding up, as it falls. An air reaction that lands ends at once, so the fighter can act on the landing tick; one that runs out in the air becomes a normal fall.

## Cancels

Specials and supers cancel an attack once its own strike has hit or been blocked: punch, kick, the crouching attacks, punch 2 and kick 2 accept the drill, IORI, DST, the axe and every super. Punch 1's and kick 1's hit branches accept them at once. The press counts on any tick, including the contact pause, as long as it is still within its command's window when the pause ends; the new move starts then. A connected air punch accepts the air kick, Air Drill, the air Getter Beam or a double jump; a connected air kick accepts the same except the air punch. Air attacks after a double jump or during an air dash cannot double-jump cancel; air dashes accept air attacks and the air specials from their fourth tick, and those attacks fall more slowly. A connected IORI stage accepts only keys 1–4: this super cancel plays the SC version, costs two stocks (NO SUPER otherwise) and flashes GENGAI!, and the hidden super's cancel plays NO SUPER at 150 of 510 life or more.

## COUNTER HIT

While a standing or crouching guard reaction plays and Ryuko has a stock, press H to COUNTER HIT: she spends the stock, freezes the attacker and swings a wide blow that launches them. The press counts at the guard reaction's recovered checks, including one up to 80 ms before the block. Air guards cannot counter.

## Throws

Press O to grab an opponent, including one holding guard. The grab checks after 70 ms: its box reaches a standing, crouching or attacking opponent on the ground up to about 167 arena pixels away, but not one airborne, turning, reeling from a hit or blocking. A miss plays a 620 ms recovery. Ryuko cannot be struck on the grab tick, so a punch landing then passes through her, and two simultaneous grabs both miss. A grab holds the opponent in front for 50 ms and spins them through three 200 ms cycles, as the Daisetsuzan's first spins do, then tosses them into the air for 30 damage. Ryuko's recovery lasts 1.4 seconds. The opponent tumbles, bounces into knockdown and gets up before control returns. The held opponent cannot be hit; the tossed one can, until knockdown (its 129–131 body boxes are kind 2). A lying fighter's body sits below the floor and a rising one has none, so neither can be hit or grabbed. Knockdown and get-up keep push boxes, so bodies still push apart. Fighters stay 75 arena pixels inside the view's edges, as the engine clamps them, so a stage end's wall is 75 pixels in; a wide pose keeps its anchor when the pose changes. A hit on the thrower before the toss frees the opponent without damage. A lethal toss KOs the victim where it was tossed; reset releases held fighters.
