# Evidence

What the port recovers from the source, with the action indices, PCs, opcodes and engine addresses behind it, and which rules are authored for training. [gameplay.md](gameplay.md) describes how the rules play.

## Recovered and authored behavior

- **Recovered behavior:** `src/game/standing.ts` ports the verified standing interpreter, including variable/collision-record assignments, branching, unsigned LCG arithmetic, timing accumulator, explicit unsupported-opcode errors, and odd-width facing placement. It executes instructions at fixed 10 ms ticks, independently of Phaser rendering. Idle action-end restarts action 1 with the continuing random seed as a new prototype choice.
- **Acceptance:** Vitest compares every event, branch, instruction, final PC, timer, and seed against 21 independently generated Python traces. The supplied seed-0 handoff timeline is also compared directly: 18 displays, nine images including 295, 223 ticks. These are comparisons against the supplied research; the original Windows game has not been run here.
- **Authored rules:** every move, object and reaction plays from its own source timeline (see Animations), and motion, body push, the hit test, the camera and round flow follow the engine routines cited below. The port authors its controls (the single keys beside the recovered commands, the Shift dash, the guard key, a ground jump on a fresh press, the touch buttons), the fighter, opponent, stage and mode choices, the practice partners and settings, the normal KO path for every lethal strike, and the music toggle; each bullet below names its own authored details.
- **Animations:** `animations.ts` loops locomotion/crouch, plays finite action and reaction sequences, and holds final airborne/ending poses. Recovered costume changes are preserved. Timing is authored where source commands wait for gameplay. Ryuko's normals (178–194), Hit Up (200–202), dashes (13–17), Jump 2 (10–12), specials and supers (see Supers) play from their own flattened timelines, as Zoma's moves do (see Ryuko's moves): Drill Punch 207 and Air Drill 208–212; the axe throw's “FIRE START” (226), whose object is “Fly Ball” 227 with impact 230; IORI 246–248; DST 214–224 with its missile objects 218; the chuck 196–199; EX Ch 204–205 and CPU IORI 410–411. - **Body push:** after each object moves, `0x40fce9` (in the motion loop `0x40f910`) walks its body slots from 19 down and, for every kind-bit-0 box overlapping one of a later object's, pushes both apart by a quarter of the overlap (`<< 14` in 16.16), in whole source pixels (`x >> 16`). - **Corner push-back:** a hit sets the victim's reaction bits (`+0x15e`) to 8 and a guard to 12 (`0x40f7a8`, `0x40f6a2`). A strike by a fighter's own box records the striker in the victim's `+0xdefd` (`0x40f8a4`), and so does an object following its parent (op `0x04` flag `0x20`, `+0x28` bit `0x20000000`, `0x40f8ac`); other objects leave it as it was. Action changes (`0x410cf0`) keep both, through a reaction's follow-ups such as knockdown, bounce and get-up. A script that runs out reaches the next action's op `0x00`, which returns the fighter to neutral (`0x4126de` → `0x411a80`) and clears bits 4 and 8 (on the ground 1 and 2 as well) and the striker (`0x411abc–0x411b06`); landing without a handler clears the bits too (`0x4124c3`). A command entered while free (bits 4 and 8 clear) sets 4 and clears 8 (`0x411a41`), as does landing into a handler such as LEND (`0x4118f3`); a cancel keeps them (`0x4119e8`), and so does op `0x0a`, which air attacks, air dashes and airborne supers use to fall into action 7. A player's motion step adds its transfer (`+0xdf49`, `0x40fa09`), then clamps it to the walls (`0x40face`); when the wall stops a victim holding both marks and not KO'd, the stopped distance joins the transfer of the striker's owner (`0x40fb08`), unless the striker is itself reeling from a hit (bits 8 without 4, `0x40faf0`). An object striker's bits stay clear, so a cornered Getter Beam victim pushes Ryuko back, and her beam (objects 269/271/273, 282, 324/327/328, all flag `0x21`) moves with her: after its own motion, a following object takes its parent's position plus the op's offset, mirrored by the parent's facing (`0x40f992–0x40f9d9`). The striker moves by it at its own next motion step, the same tick for fighter 1 and the next for fighter 0. A cornered kick or guard therefore ends at the open-stage gap with the striker giving up the slide, and a victim launched into the wall pushes the striker away through its bounce and floor slide. In the Vs. modes the walls are 50 and 590 source pixels from the camera (see Stage/camera), and the left one measures the stopped distance as (50 − camera) − x (`0x40fbdd`) rather than from the wall, so once the camera has scrolled it hands on nothing. A contact sets the bits only when its box deals damage (byte `+0xc`), so a grab (196, 214) records the thrower as striker without them. Ops `0x07` and `0x14` then mark the held or tossed victim reeling and held (`+0x15e & ~5 | 0xa`, `0x413020`, `0x41314a`) and keep the striker. Op `0x07` places the victim once, before its display, unless its bit 4 (`0x10`, DST's fast spins 217) has the holder's motion step re-place it every tick (`0x40fa29`), after the holder's transfer and before its walls. Neither op `0x07` nor `0x14` clamps the victim; its own motion step does, so a victim moving earlier in the pass than a re-placing holder (fighter 0 held by fighter 1) shows unclamped until the holder takes the overflow. A cornered thrower is therefore pushed out as the spins swing its victim behind it into the wall: by the 15 and 75 pixels the −10 and −60 holds lose, after which the −60 hold meets the wall exactly.

## Recovered contact timing

A strike's active/inactive cycle uses **action ticks**. Successful contact adds **hit-stop**: the fighter's action age, airborne age, position, velocity, and acceleration stop advancing for the specified ticks. This is separate from the victim's subsequent hit/block recovery. One tick is 10 ms in this port. Whiffs add no pause. Effects, the round clock and the input history continue, so chain and cancel presses made during the pause count once it ends, while still in their windows. After a KO or time-out the round phase stays 1 (only a debug key sets 2, `0x406231` and `0x408f38`): moves, objects, commands and the CPU run on, and each fighter takes its result's action once back in neutral (`0x410ff1`); only the timer stops (`0x408e50`).
