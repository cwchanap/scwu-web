# SCWU Web MVP

A local Ryuko mirror match on Sport Court, built with TypeScript, Svelte 5, Phaser 3, Vite, and Vitest. Includes free practice, the original CPU opponent recovered from Ryuko's CPU table, forward/backward movement, dashes, double jumps, standing/crouching/aerial attacks and guard, three-step standing attack chains, Drill Punch, Hit Up launcher and jump chase, the three-part IORI chain, Axe throw, close-range throws, the Daisetsuzan Oroshi command throw, a power gauge with three stocks, Ryuko's five supers (Getter Beam, Musashi, Getter Change Attack, Black Getter Mega Rush, Mega Getter Beam) and NO SUPER, knockdowns and get-ups, hit/block reactions, KO/victory/draw animations, health, a 60-second CPU round, pause, reset, and touch controls.

## Run

Use Bun 1.3.9+, Node 24.12+ for the tooling CLIs, and Python 3.10+. Keep the supplied `scwu-web-handoff/` directory beside `package.json`.

```sh
bun install
bun run dev
```

Open the localhost address Vite prints. `predev` converts only the required art with the handoff's standard-library Python decoder. Missing or changed Ryuko source data produces an explicit error. No Python, Windows runtime, original executable, or backend is needed by the browser.

```sh
bun run validate # export assets, formatting, ESLint, types, Vitest
bun run build    # export assets, types, production build
bun run preview  # serve the production build locally
bun run format  # apply Prettier
```

`bun run test` and `bun run check` expect assets to have been exported once (`bun run assets`). Husky's pre-commit hook runs `bun run validate`; `bun install` installs the hook when this directory is a Git checkout. Use `bun install --frozen-lockfile` for reproducible installs from `bun.lock`. Run `bun run test` to use Vitest; `bun test` selects Bun’s built-in test runner. No commit or remote is created automatically.

Free practice has no timer. Below the arena, choose the partner's behavior: stand still, hold a standing or crouching guard, jump and guard in the air, punch once a second when you are in range, back away and jump in with an air kick, or play the original CPU (the same one as Vs. CPU, without the round timer). **Max stock** tops each fighter up to three stocks whenever they stand idle, and **Low health** holds Ryuko at 30% health for the desperation moves. These settings apply at once and persist across resets. The partner uses the same rules as the player; apart from the original CPU, its behaviors are authored for testing. Vs. CPU plays the original game's CPU. Every half second or less, it picks a move from Ryuko's recovered CPU table by distance, odds and who is airborne: walks, jumps, dashes, punch and kick strings, Hit Up, the specials, the supers (NO SUPER without stock) and its own two-strike IORI. It blocks about half of the hits that reach it while it is free to guard. Each Vs. CPU round opens with Ryuko's recovered walk-in and transformation intro (1.99 seconds) for both fighters; input, the CPU, and the round clock start after it, and buttons held through the intro must be pressed again. Changing modes resets the round. Leaving the window pauses the match and clears held input; resume explicitly. Touch buttons support simultaneous movement and actions. Landscape is recommended on phones.

Hold crouch while attacking or guarding for low poses; attack or guard during a jump for aerial poses. Every attack strikes from its recovered hitbox, and every pose is hit through its own recovered body box. Centre to centre against a standing opponent, the standing punch and kick reach about 260 and 280 arena pixels, the air kick about 150, and Kick 3's beam about 550. Crouching or walking narrows the body, so attacks reach a little less; a backdash cannot be hit once it leaves the ground, and a grab cannot be struck on its grab tick. Life is the source's 510 points. A hit takes the striking box's damage, 20% more against most attacking poses. A guard takes nothing, except 20% chip from IORI, the drills, the axe and most super boxes, and chip can KO. At 30% life (153) or less, every loss shrinks to 60%. When the opponent crosses over, a grounded fighter plays the recovered 100 ms standing or crouching turn before walking or idling again; any other input interrupts it. The crouching kick sweeps: a hit pops the victim into a short knockdown and get-up. Attacks finish before another can start, except through the recovered chains and cancels below. Landing interrupts aerial attacks, and an air hit or air guard ends the moment it lands. Airborne fighters land before their round-result pose. KO plays the recovered lettering sequence for 166 ticks (1.66 seconds), followed by one second with the winner visible. Rematch controls appear below the court, keeping the ending poses unobscured.

Punch and kick chain only after contact. When punch 1 or kick 1 hits or is blocked, it switches to its hit branch; 180 ms later (the 100 ms contact pause plus the branch's first 80 ms pose) the branch looks back 200 ms (250 ms for the kick) for a fresh punch or kick press made after a moment with no direction held. Punch enters punch 2 and kick enters kick 2, so the chains cross. A whiff has no follow-up. Punch 2 checks the same way after each of its three strikes, and kick 2 once after its strike; a press there cancels at once into punch 3 or kick 3, even on a whiff. Punch 2 and kick 2 open with a lunge of about 117 and 99 arena pixels, which carries them back into range after the victim's slide. The second standing punch and jumping kick each have three strikes; the third standing kick has eight. Each strike has its own active window and hit/block effect, with inactive gaps between strikes. Their total damage stays at 10, 13, and 17 respectively. Holding an attack never repeats it, and a hit on the attacker ends its chain. Jump direction is chosen when jump is pressed: neutral jumps rise about 318 arena pixels, while forward/backward jumps rise about 296 and travel about 348 pixels when unobstructed. Jump travel continues after releasing direction and through aerial attacks or guard. Double jumps pause for 30 ms before launching in the newly selected direction. These velocities and acceleration use recovered actions 4–6 and 10–12, converted from the original fixed-point units to the arena scale. Each flight allows one double jump and one air dash, refreshed on landing. On the ground, two fresh taps of the same direction within 200 ms trigger a dash on keyboard or touch; opposite input, simultaneous left/right, pause, and focus loss clear the sequence. In the air, one fresh left/right press triggers a dash in that direction on keyboard or touch. Holding a direction through takeoff keeps the normal jump: release and press again to air dash. Simultaneous left/right does not select a dash direction, and the one-dash-per-flight limit still applies. These input choices, including the 200 ms ground window, belong to training mode. Dash direction follows the held movement direction, or goes toward the opponent by default. Ground dashes launch after 50/100 ms of preparation and hold their airborne pose until landing. Actions 13–14 set forward/backward velocities of +900/-500, upward velocities of -500/-900, and gravity 90 in source units. Unobstructed hops cover about 243/247 arena pixels in 180/330 ms, respectively; the backward hop is higher and slower. Air dashes retain the incoming jump or fall motion during their 30 ms preparation; if that fall reaches the floor, they land before launching. At launch they replace it with level motion at about 12 arena pixels per tick: 300 ms of level travel forward (about 360 pixels), or 200 ms backward (about 240 pixels). Their 100 ms recovery restores gravity while keeping horizontal velocity; that drift continues during the fall and aerial attacks/guard until landing or another move replaces it. Actions 16–17 supply velocity ±800 and gravity 80; the recovery motion flag `0x02` retains horizontal velocity, as does the following fall action 7. A forward air dash near the neutral-jump apex covers about 720 arena pixels through landing when unobstructed. The 960-pixel viewport scrolls over a 1920-pixel stage, so crossing the old screen edge no longer cuts off the move. Actual stage walls and body collision can shorten travel. Ground Drill Punch lunges from action ticks 11–59 (110–590 ms before contact pauses) at about 13.5 arena pixels per tick, slides at about 1.5 pixels per tick until action tick 69, then stops for its remaining recovery. Contact pauses stop this movement clock as well as the poses. Body collisions and arena walls limit its travel. It advances through six timed strikes for 3 damage each (18 total), with hit reactions and sparks on every contact; the sixth strike launches the victim. Guard blocks all six strikes. The aerial drill holds its 220 ms startup, then dives forward at about 12 arena pixels per tick and downward at about 15; follow-up stages increase the downward speed to about 22.5. It spins until landing. Hit or block advances its contact stages; a missed spin stays armed. Damage drops from 16 to 5, 3, then 1 per subsequent contact, so its hit count depends on contact and airtime; a 1-damage spin that passes without contact returns to 3. A hit slams the victim down into a floor bounce, and an airborne victim is struck again by each stage until it lands. The attacker holds position for eight contact ticks, then moves through the two transition ticks before the next strike. Landing ends the attack and plays its recovery. Its spinning box reaches about 189 arena pixels ahead. Axe throw releases a spinning axe 160 ms in, flying at about 13.5 arena pixels per tick; a new throw removes the thrower's previous axe. It hits through body boxes like any attack (36 against an attacking pose), so it passes through a fighter mid-throw and under a high enough jump, and it can be blocked. Two opposing axes break each other where they meet, the Mega Getter Beam's beam breaks every opposing axe, and its lunge meeting an axe enters its hit cinematic. A super's freeze holds a flying axe in place without its hitbox, and an axe vanishes 75 pixels past either end of the stage. These two basic specials are meter-free.

Press H or the Hit Up touch button on the ground for an upward kick. It steps forward about 120 arena pixels, strikes after 150 ms of action time, and deals 15 damage. A grounded hit pauses the attacker for 330 ms and the victim for 100 ms; the victim then holds the impact pose for 250 ms before rising about 589 arena pixels and falling into knockdown/get-up. Tap Jump during startup, the contact pause, or early successful-hit recovery to chase them, then use an aerial attack. The chase prepares for 80 ms and preserves its forward momentum in the air. Blocking or missing plays the full recovery without a chase. An airborne victim instead takes a shorter hold and a stronger outward launch. The camera stays locked to the player: launching the opponent does not pan the view, while chasing them follows your own movement. The HUD stays fixed. The H binding, early Jump buffering, and camera following are training choices.

Press F or the IORI touch button on the ground for a three-part advancing attack. Tap again during the first stage to queue the second, then again during the second for the finisher. Early taps, including during hit-stop, are remembered; each follow-up begins after the current strike ends. Holding the button performs one stage. You can continue on hit, block, or whiff, but interruption clears the queue. Damage is 15 + 18 + 20 (53 total); a guard takes 3 + 3 + 4 chip. The first grounded hit pushes away, the second pulls inward, and the finisher launches a grounded victim away or slams an airborne victim into a bounce and knockdown. Without follow-up input, each stage completes its recovery. Once a stage connects, a super cancels it (see below). The web binding, input buffering, ranges, and meter-free access are training choices.

Press G or the Daisetsuzan touch button on the ground for Ryuko's command throw. 40 ms into the move, its grab box reaches an opponent up to about 259 arena pixels away, including one reeling from a hit, but not one blocking or turning. A miss plays a 580 ms recovery. On a grab, Ryuko spins the opponent overhead for 1.8 seconds, then hurls them into the sky for 10 damage. As they fall, Ryuko slides back and fires two missiles that catch them for 22 damage each. The missile hit spins them away into a bounce, knockdown, and get-up. The held opponent cannot be hit. If time runs out during the spin, the opponent is released without the toss. A lethal toss still finishes Ryuko's sequence before the victory pose. At 30% health or less, G performs EVIL DST instead: the same grab and miss recovery with its own poses, then five held strikes (1.65 seconds, 25 power, no damage) before the usual spin, toss and missiles.

Hits, blocks, and damage taken fill the power gauge at the bottom of the screen. Each full gauge becomes a stock, up to three; there is no free meter in practice. Keys 1–5 or the gold touch buttons spend stock on the supers. Without enough stock, Ryuko plays NO SUPER for 400 ms and gains 10 power instead. Each super freezes the opponent while Ryuko transforms. Getter Beam fires a screen-long beam that hits 20 times for 100 damage; a guard takes 1 chip from each. Pinned against a wall, the opponent's slides push Ryuko back, and the beam moves with her. At 30% health or less it becomes the stronger desperation beam (120 damage). In the air, Ryuko hovers while firing, then falls. Musashi and the Getter Change Attack are unblockable grabs, though both pass over an opponent still reeling from a guard, and the Change Attack also over one in the air or reeling from a hit: Musashi lifts the opponent overhead and slams them for 120 damage. Cancelled from IORI, Musashi also catches an opponent in the air. The Change Attack repeats the Daisetsuzan spin, tosses the opponent, catches them in the sky, throws them and fires a beam as they fall. Key 4 throws an axe until Ryuko's health falls below 30%, then performs the seven-strike Black Getter Mega Rush for 125 damage. The Mega Getter Beam needs all three stocks: its opening blast shocks the opponent, then a cinematic beam from the sky hits 40 times; 240 damage in all. A hit on Ryuko cancels her super, including a beam already on screen.

Every attack chooses its victim's reaction from the recovered response table: standing, crouching or airborne, each guarded or not. Air punch and air kick are overheads, hitting a crouching guard and pushing a standing guard back. Punch 3 slams any grounded victim into a floor bounce, even through a crouching guard; once the bounce starts, attacks that reach its low body (kicks, crouching attacks, IORI) strike the victim again. An air guard stops only aerial attacks, punch 3, IORI, the drill and the axe; the ground normals and Hit Up hit it. Each grounded reaction then slides as in the source: a light hit about 84 arena pixels, a heavy hit 214, an air attack's hit 71, a light block 96 and a heavy block about 204. A beam hit (kick 3, Getter Beam) pushes the victim back for 25 ticks, then drifts it in again: about 12 pixels between kick 3's strikes and 18 between Getter Beam's. Air hits rise briefly, then fly the victim up and away; an air guard slides back, speeding up, as it falls. An air reaction that lands ends at once, so the fighter can act on the landing tick; one that runs out in the air becomes a normal fall.

Specials and supers cancel an attack once its own strike has hit or been blocked: punch, kick, the crouching attacks, punch 2 and kick 2 accept the drill, IORI, DST, the axe and every super. Punch 1's and kick 1's hit branches accept them at once. The press counts on any tick, including the contact pause; the new move starts when the pause ends. A connected air punch accepts the air kick, Air Drill, the air Getter Beam or a double jump; a connected air kick accepts the same except the air punch. Air attacks after a double jump or during an air dash cannot double-jump cancel; air dashes accept air attacks and the air specials from their third tick, and those attacks fall more slowly. A connected IORI stage accepts only keys 1–4: this super cancel plays the SC version, costs two stocks (NO SUPER otherwise) and flashes GENGAI!, and the hidden super's cancel plays NO SUPER at 150 of 510 life or more.

While a standing or crouching guard reaction plays and Ryuko has a stock, press H to COUNTER HIT: she spends the stock, freezes the attacker and swings a wide blow that launches them. The press counts at the guard reaction's recovered checks, including one up to 80 ms before the block. Air guards cannot counter.

Press O to grab an opponent, including one holding guard. The grab checks after 70 ms: its box reaches a standing, crouching or attacking opponent on the ground up to about 167 arena pixels away, but not one airborne, turning, reeling from a hit or blocking. A miss plays a 620 ms recovery. Ryuko cannot be struck on the grab tick, so a punch landing then passes through her, and two simultaneous grabs both miss. A grab holds the opponent in front for 50 ms and spins them through three 200 ms cycles, as the Daisetsuzan's first spins do, then tosses them into the air for 30 damage. Ryuko's recovery lasts 1.4 seconds. The opponent tumbles, bounces into knockdown and gets up before control returns. The held opponent cannot be hit; the tossed one can, until knockdown (its 129–131 body boxes are kind 2). A lying fighter's body sits below the floor and a rising one has none, so neither can be hit or grabbed. Knockdown and get-up keep push boxes, so bodies still push apart. Fighter anchors stay 185 arena pixels from either stage edge so the extended air dash and wide throw/recovery poses remain visible without shifting position when the pose changes. A hit on the thrower before the toss frees the opponent without damage. Lethal throws finish on the floor without getting up; reset and timeout also release held fighters.

## Evidence and deliberate limits

- **Recovered behavior:** `src/game/standing.ts` ports the verified standing interpreter, including variable/collision-record assignments, branching, unsigned LCG arithmetic, timing accumulator, explicit unsupported-opcode errors, and odd-width facing placement. It executes instructions at fixed 10 ms ticks, independently of Phaser rendering. Idle action-end restarts action 1 with the continuing random seed as a new prototype choice.
- **Acceptance:** Vitest compares every event, branch, instruction, final PC, timer, and seed against 21 independently generated Python traces. The supplied seed-0 handoff timeline is also compared directly: 18 displays, nine images including 295, 223 ticks. These are comparisons against the supplied research; the original Windows game has not been run here.
- **New rules:** `match.ts` defines prototype movement, gravity, body separation, guard, hitstun, and round flow. Non-idle animation clips use deliberately selected display poses and offsets from the containers; they do not claim to execute those actions' original bytecode, physics, collisions, or transitions. CPU uses the same Ryuko art. Camera/crop is a new presentation choice.
- **Animations:** `animations.ts` loops locomotion/crouch, plays finite action and reaction sequences, and holds final airborne/ending poses. Recovered costume changes are preserved. Timing is authored where source commands wait for gameplay. The movement expansion selects actions 10, 13–17; follow-ups use 180–181 and 188–190; Drill Punch uses 207–212; Axe throw uses the source's “FIRE START” and “Fly Ball” actions 226–227 and impact 230. The third kick repeats action 190 eight times, matching the recovered loop count. Aerial drill follows the contact-dependent stages of actions 209–211 rather than playing a fixed number of spin cycles; a spin of 211 that passes without contact jumps back to 210 (PC 3060). Jump, dash, and drill movement use recovered opcode-1 velocities at source speed 10, converted to the web art scale. Air-dash entry preserves airborne velocity until the first motion command (PCs 205/223); the original action-change path at `0x410d50` clears motion only on the ground. Opcode `0x1e` controls action cancellation eligibility, not dash acceleration. Per-contact fighter pauses use recovered response timings described below. The full gameplay VM remains unimplemented; Hit Up, IORI, and the normal KO use the recovered motion described below. Kick 3 now uses the source BEAM hit and no-time-lack guard pose sequences (83–84, 163, 165, 167). Victim reactions follow the recovered responses and motion described below. While its axe (slot 6) lives, flying or breaking, axe throw 226's op `0x04` at PC 3267 jumps to action 225, which shows one display and returns to neutral, so the axe flies on; PC 3279 would likewise jump to punch 178 rather than spawn a second, which can't happen once 226 has started without one. With EX mode's player variable 0 at 10, PC 3266 enters EX Fire (231) instead; the port has no EX mode. Sixteen ticks in, 226 spawns action 227 at (−6, −102) (PC 3279). Action 227 moves at raw 900 (PC 3293) and re-sets box (63, 3, ±39, ±44; kind 6, damage 30) every 3-tick display; handlers 2 and 3 (hit, guard) enter impact 230. Action 227 loops until deleted: `0x4124c9` deletes an object that doesn't follow its parent once it leaves x −50..1330 or y −50..1010 source pixels (the floor is y 920, `0x412247`), the DST missiles too. After each display, 227's op `0x1f` (PCs 3297–3318) checks global 6 (`0x4456bc`, selector `0x86`): while it is above 0, the axe zeroes its motion and box and replays that display. Supers and COUNTER HIT add their freeze to global 6 beside their op `0x1a` stops (Getter Beam 10, then 65 at age 5), and KGT action 58, the stage layout, counts it down every tick except while paused (PCs 366–371), so an axe holds from its next display through the first after the freeze. Each tick the engine sorts objects into 128 priority buckets (`+4`) and runs their scripts bucket by bucket, slot by slot (`0x404d0b–0x404d89`), before the hit test and motion. KGT objects spawn at `0x65` (`0x406890`'s callers), after the fighters (`0x50`) and the axe and missiles (op `0x04` flag 1, one in front of their spawner; 0 behind, 2 from byte 13), so the countdown follows every read. The DST missile (218, PC 3168) holds one display only when global 6 is exactly 1. The axe's slot-1 box and slot-0 body (PCs 3290–3291) repeat its box 2000 pixels down, where only objects meet: `0x40f010` tests every object's boxes against the other side's bodies, and an object victim (`+0x15a` 1) only fires the striker's hit handler (`0x40f36a`, taken up at its next script step) and marks the striker as having hit (`+0x15e` bit `0x10`, `0x40f31c`). `0x40f010` checks that bit only before a striker's pass, and every display step clears it for an object holding a box of kind bit 1 (`0x41257d`), players included, and op `0x18` setting such a box clears it for a player (`0x412d49`). In Ryuko's data that layer holds the axe's box and body, the beams' boxes (270, 277: (644, 1995) ±(644, 72)), the Mega Getter Beam's lunge box (292: (59, 1908) ±(40, 89), hit handler 294 from PC 4311) and its beam's bodies (303, 304: (0, 1995) ±(10000, 182)). So two opposing axes break into 230 together, without damage, stop or effect, even while held; that beam's bodies break every opposing axe; its lunge enters 294 on an axe; and a beam, which sets no handler, only stops hitting until its next box. The lunge records the axe as caught (`+0xdef9`, `0x40f2e0`), but 294 has no op `0x07` or `0x14`: only grab-entered actions (197–199, 215–223, 257–259, 320–322) and Air Drill's hit branch (209, PC 2997) hold or toss, so the axe flies on. A script that runs out reaches the next action's op `0x00`, which deletes an object (`0x41273c`, `0x413bc6`); `0x4124c9` also culls one that leaves the world unless it follows a parent. Back in neutral, `0x410ff1` enters the action word of the round's result (`+0xe007`: 1 win 57, 2 lose 59, 3 draw 65), and the result states (`0x409444`, `0x409519`) give the loser 3 once the timer (`0x470050`) has run out, so a time-out loser takes the draw action, WIN STAND and the seated pose. Turns use actions 23/24 (ten ticks, mirrored mid-turn pose). The Vs. CPU intro follows action 30's path for global 3 = 0 (one human player, PC 286): action 32 jumps to action 33 at PC 333, skipping the blank repositioning displays. Raw forward velocity 200 lasts until PC 339 (50 ticks, about 150 arena pixels), then the transformation poses finish at 199 ticks. Its walk-in endpoints, 360 pixels apart around the stage center, and skipping the intro in free practice are authored. Win and draw call WIN STAND (action 58, 115 ticks) through opcode `0x09` at PCs 565/667 before their own poses. Crouching kick response 7 selects action 79: the victim holds three 2-tick impact poses, launches at raw velocity −300/−1020 with gravity 90, and lands into knockdown 146 and get-up.

- **Body push:** after each object moves, `0x40fce9` (in the motion loop `0x40f910`) walks its body slots from 19 down and, for every kind-bit-0 box overlapping one of a later object's, pushes both apart by a quarter of the overlap (`<< 14` in 16.16), in whole source pixels (`x >> 16`). Against a grounded fighter only the airborne one moves (`0x40fec3`), a KO'd fighter (life `+0xdf05` is 0) pushes no one, and hit stop skips motion but not the push. Held, holding and hidden poses (the held victims, 197, 215, 222, 294 and the shocked victim) set no push box, so those pairs overlap. A walker shoves an idle opponent along at half its speed; the drill's forward box (44, −74) ±(38, 86) holds its victim about 164 arena pixels ahead, and kick 3's closing leg box (57, −56) ±(57, 65) shoves a guard's slide about 39 pixels further. The motion loop takes fighter 0, then fighter 1: each moves, adds its transfer and clamps (see Corner push-back), and fighter 0 pushes in between, against fighter 1's boxes from the previous tick. Fighter 0's share stays unclamped until its next motion step (a jumper falling onto a body at the left wall hangs about 26 pixels past it, and the body, moving after the push, turns to face it), and a box fighter 1 opens meets fighter 0 a tick late (getting up against the wall, the walker is eased back 13.5 pixels at once).
- **Corner push-back:** a hit sets the victim's reaction bits (`+0x15e`) to 8 and a guard to 12 (`0x40f7a8`, `0x40f6a2`). A strike by a fighter's own box records the striker in the victim's `+0xdefd` (`0x40f8a4`), and so does an object following its parent (op `0x04` flag `0x20`, `+0x28` bit `0x20000000`, `0x40f8ac`); other objects leave it as it was. Action changes (`0x410cf0`) keep both, through a reaction's follow-ups such as knockdown, bounce and get-up. A script that runs out reaches the next action's op `0x00`, which returns the fighter to neutral (`0x4126de` → `0x411a80`) and clears bits 4 and 8 (on the ground 1 and 2 as well) and the striker (`0x411abc–0x411b06`); landing without a handler clears the bits too (`0x4124c3`). ## Recovered contact timing

A strike's active/inactive cycle uses **action ticks**. Successful contact adds **hit-stop**: the fighter's action age, airborne age, position, velocity, and acceleration stop advancing for the specified ticks. This is separate from the victim's subsequent hit/block recovery. One tick is 10 ms in this port. Whiffs add no pause. Effects, the round clock and the input history continue, so chain and cancel presses count during the pause. KO ends combat immediately and lets the final contact pause finish before the fighter's ending playback.

Each pause comes from the selected reaction's own `0x1a`, so stance-dependent responses (overheads, air guards) change the pause with the reaction. Aerial drill has a separate two-tick moving transition after its eight-tick attacker pause. Its victim resumes at different times on hit versus block, so subsequent contacts depend on their relative positions, not a fixed hit count.

Ground drill still strikes at action ages 23, 29, 35, 41, 47, 53. With every strike connecting immediately, the actual contact gaps are now **210 ms on hit** or **160 ms on block**, versus **60 ms for Kick 3**. Punch 2's first two gaps are 160 ms, and aerial kick's are 230 ms. The final strike's pause delays recovery rather than adding another strike.

Evidence: the character container tail contains 23 command entries of 82 bytes, followed by 83 four-byte response mappings at file offset `0x1576d9d`. Opcode `0x17` selects a mapping, and reaction opcode `0x1a` adds its first two bytes to the self/other fighter stop counters. The original engine handles this at `0x413637`/`0x4136ae`; `0x412390` skips animation advancement and `0x40f963` skips motion while stopped. The KGT base hit/guard stop bytes loaded at `0x43a299`/`0x43a29a` are both zero. This is static recovery from the supplied files, not original Windows playback verification. Source propagation to auxiliary objects/effects remains outside the port's fighter pause implementation.
